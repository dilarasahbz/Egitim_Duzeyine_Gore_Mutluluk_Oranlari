TÜİK Happiness Analysis by Education Level

Applied Statistics Project — Technical Documentation & Academic Report

1. Project Overview

Based on official household survey microdata published by the Turkish Statistical Institute (TÜİK) from 2004 to 2022, this project empirically investigates subjective well-being across educational attainment cohorts (Happy, Moderate, Unhappy) and models the statistical impact of education level on happiness scores using parametric and non-parametric methodologies.

Core Analytical Objectives

  • Benchmark happiness distributions across Higher Education, High School, Primary/Middle School, and Did Not Complete School cohorts.
  • Examine longitudinal emotional trend shifts over an 18-year horizon using comparative hypothesis testing.
  • Formulate a Multiple Linear Regression model to quantify the explanatory power of education tiers on continuous well-being scores.

4. Inferential Statistics and Hypothesis Testing

4.1. Normality Assessment

Normality for Higher Education cohorts was checked via Density Plots, Q-Q Plots, and the Shapiro-Wilk test (H₀: Data follows a normal distribution vs. H₁: Data does not follow a normal distribution):

Yuksekogretim_2004 <- projeson |> filter(Yil == 2004) |> select(Yuksekogretim)
shapiro.test(Yuksekogretim_2004$Yuksekogretim)

Result: With p < 0.05, H₀ was rejected. Although formal parametric assumptions were violated, two-sample Student's T-tests were retained for comparative evaluation.

4.2. Two-Sample Mean Comparison (Student's T-Test)

Emotional state averages of Higher Education graduates were compared between 2004 and 2022 (H₀: μ₂₀₀₄ = μ₂₀₂₂):

t.test(x = projeson$Yuksekogretim[projeson$Yil == 2004],
       y = projeson$Yuksekogretim[projeson$Yil == 2022],
       alternative = "two.sided", var.equal = TRUE)

Result: Since p > 0.05, H₀ cannot be rejected. At the 5% significance level, no statistically significant difference is observed between 2004 and 2022 mean emotional scores.

4.3. Population Proportion Testing

One-Sample Proportion Test

Evaluated whether the 2022 Higher Education happiness proportion equaled 0.5 via prop.test(). The test yielded p < 0.05, confirming that the difference is statistically significant.

Two-Sample Proportion Test

Hypothesis H₁: p₂₀₂₂ > p₂₀₀₄ confirmed that the 2022 Higher Education "moderate" emotional state proportion (0.387) was significantly greater than the 2004 proportion (0.256) (p < 0.05).

6. Variance Comparison with F-Distribution

Equality of variances between "Happy" and "Unhappy" responses was assessed for Higher Education graduates (H₀: σ²_Happy = σ²_Unhappy):

test <- var.test(x = Mutlu, y = Mutsuz, ratio = 1, alternative = "two.sided")
# F = 5.6631, p-value = 0.1216

Evaluation: Since p = 0.1216 > 0.05, H₀ cannot be rejected. There is no statistically significant difference in variance between the two emotional states.

8. Conclusion and General Evaluation

  • Across all analyses conducted on the TÜİK survey microdata, a statistically significant relationship was established between educational attainment tiers and reported emotional well-being distributions.
  • Chi-Square independence tests demonstrate that both education levels and longitudinal survey periods exert a statistically dependent effect on emotional distributions.
  • The Multiple Linear Regression model explains 98.74% of the variance in aggregate happiness indices, identifying completion rates in Higher Education (p ≈ 0.044) and Primary School (p ≈ 0.021) as the primary statistically significant determinants.
